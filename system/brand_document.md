## 2. Mission

Teach anyone to build real, deployable computer vision and AI applications — starting from the very basics, using step-by-step project-based learning, without requiring prior expertise.

CVZone believes in learning by building. Every course, every project, and every email should reinforce this: **you learn by doing, not by reading theory**.

## 3. Target Audience

### Primary Audience
- **Python developers** (beginner to intermediate) who want to build practical AI and CV applications
- **Makers and hobbyists** interested in hardware + software projects (Arduino, Raspberry Pi, drones, robots)
- **Students** at school or university level studying programming, AI, or robotics
- **Freelancers** who want to build commercial computer vision products to sell

### Secondary Audience
- **Educators and institutions** looking to teach AI and robotics with structured, hands-on curricula
- **Engineers and professionals** adding computer vision skills to their existing expertise
- **Career changers** entering the AI/computer vision field from other programming backgrounds

### Audience Mindset
The CVZone audience is **curious, action-oriented, and practical**. They don't want to read 300 pages before building something. They want to see it work. They are excited by AI and robotics but often feel intimidated — CVZone's job is to remove that intimidation and replace it with momentum.

They are motivated by:
- Seeing real results fast
- Building projects they can show off
- Not wasting money on tools that don't work
- Being part of a trend before it goes mainstream (AI, robotics, edge AI)

## 4. Brand Voice

### The Core Voice: Encouraging Expert

Write like a knowledgeable friend who genuinely wants you to succeed — confident, direct, and excited about the technology, but never condescending or overly corporate.

### Voice Characteristics

**Direct and confident**
Say things clearly. Don't hedge or over-qualify. CVZone copy doesn't say "you might potentially learn to build AI apps." It says "you will build 10 real AI apps."

**Encouraging and inclusive**
The audience is often intimidated by the subject matter. Always make them feel capable. Reinforce that they don't need to be an expert to start.

**Excited about technology — but grounded**
CVZone is genuinely enthusiastic about AI, robotics, and computer vision. That excitement is real and should come through in emails. But it's always grounded in what you can actually build, not hype.

**Practical and results-focused**
Lead with what the reader will be able to do, build, or achieve — not with abstract descriptions of the technology. Outcomes first, explanations second.

**Conversational but professional**
Not corporate. Not casual to the point of being unprofessional. The tone is like a YouTube tutorial that happens to also be really well structured.

## 6. Writing Rules

### Always Do

- **Start with value** — the reader should know what they're getting within the first sentence
- **Use "you" and "we"** — direct address creates connection
- **Lead with outcomes** — "You will build a face recognition door lock" not "This module covers face recognition"
- **Use short sentences** — especially for key points and CTAs
- **Be specific** — "10+ real-world projects" beats "many projects"
- **Reference real tech** — YOLO, OpenCV, Python, Raspberry Pi, Arduino. This audience knows these names and it builds credibility
- **Show the transformation** — from where they are now to where they'll be after the course
- **Include social proof naturally** — "#1 ranked on Google and YouTube", "1M+ views", "100% positive feedback"
- **Match the course energy** — a drone course email should feel more adventurous than a web dev course email

### Never Do

- **Never use corporate jargon** — avoid words like "leverage", "synergy", "empower", "unlock your potential"
- **Never over-promise** — do not say "become an expert in 3 days" or "master AI overnight"
- **Never be condescending** — never imply the reader should already know something
- **Never use passive voice** as the default — "you will build" not "you will be taught to build"
- **Never ignore design** — CVZone always mentions that students learn to build elegant, modern UIs alongside functionality. Don't write emails that only mention code
- **Never write long walls of text** — short paragraphs, bullet points for lists, white space matters
- **Never skip the CTA** — every email must have a clear, single primary action

## 9. Pricing & Offer Guidelines

### How to Present Pricing
- Always show the **original price crossed out** alongside the sale price — this makes the value obvious
- Use specific numbers — "$97" not "under $100"
- Mention lifetime access whenever pricing is discussed — it is a key differentiator
- For tiered plans, always name the most popular or recommended plan

### Urgency Rules
- Only use countdown language for **actual sales** with a real end date
- Do not manufacture false urgency on non-sale emails
- For pre-order/launch emails, "launching soon" or "early bird pricing" is appropriate urgency language
- The 3-day money-back guarantee should be mentioned in any email that discusses pricing

### Refund Policy Note
The 3-day refund applies to full-price purchases. It does **not** apply during sales or to pre-orders. Do not imply otherwise in emails.

## 12. CTA (Call To Action) Guidelines

### Primary CTA Rules
- One primary CTA per email — do not dilute with multiple competing buttons
- CTA text should be **action words**: "Enroll Now", "Watch the Demo", "Get Early Access", "Start Building"
- Avoid vague CTAs: "Click Here", "Learn More" (acceptable only as secondary CTA)
- The CTA button should reflect the campaign type:

| Campaign Type | CTA Text |

### CTA URL Format
Always link to the specific course page, not the homepage:
`https://www.computervision.zone/[course-id]/`

## 13. Email Structure Principles

### Subject Lines
- Keep under 50 characters where possible
- Lead with the outcome or the hook — not the course name
- Use numbers when available: "Build 10 AI Projects on a $50 Board"
- A/B test curiosity vs. direct: "This runs AI with no internet" vs. "AI with Raspberry Pi — Now Live"
- Avoid clickbait — CVZone's audience is technical and will see through it

### Preview Text (Pre-header)
- Should complement the subject line, not repeat it
- 85–100 characters ideal
- Give one more reason to open

### Email Length by Campaign Type
| Campaign Type | Ideal Length |
|---------------|-------------|
| Course Launch | Medium-Long (full component sequence) |
| Sale | Short-Medium (urgency, price, CTA) |
| Lesson Announcement | Short (demo, what you'll learn, CTA) |
| YouTube Promotion | Short (hook, video preview, CTA) |
| AI/Robotics Update | Medium (context, relevance, CTA) |
